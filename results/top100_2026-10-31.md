# The hundred best books in the artifact store

Date of record: the last day of October, two thousand twenty-six. Source:
every artifact under `results/runs/` -- two thousand nine hundred and fifty-five files across
thirty-one adapters -- reduced to one row per measured book
(adapter x strategy x symbol x timeframe).

## How this list is built and ordered

Four rules, applied in this order:

1. **One artifact per book.** Re-runs of the same book are collapsed;
   the representative is the reproducible artifact with the widest
   window, never the highest return, so the collapse cannot cherry-pick.
2. **A sample floor of thirty closed trades** (the v1 gate floor), a
   finite profit factor above one, a positive net return, a drawdown
   below ruin and no liquidated trade.
3. **Reproducibility is a hard filter.** A book is listed only if its
   artifact re-ran bit-for-bit today under `vrun.py pverify`. Books
   whose data files have since grown (STALE) or whose engine bytes have
   changed (REFUSED) are not listed, however good they look.
4. **Ordering.** The delta-neutral XRP grid is first because it is the
   only book in the repository that passes every gate; every other book
   follows in descending profit factor, ties broken by trade count.

The cost basis is not uniform: every campaign records its own fee and
slippage in basis points inside the artifact, and across this table they
run from a fraction of a point to twenty, so profit factors are only
strictly comparable within a campaign.

The hundred rows below come from forty-three distinct strategies;
the same strategy appears several times when it made money on more than
one market, and each of those cells is a separate measurement.

## What this table does not say

- **Profit factor is not a verdict.** A high factor on a small sample is
  noise, and the verdict column records what the campaign that produced
  the book concluded. Only the first row passes the gates.
- **Small samples sit at the top.** The highest factors in the list come
  from books with a few dozen trades, several of them carrying drawdowns
  above seventy percent; the factor is real arithmetic, not evidence of
  an edge.
- **The tail is the noise floor.** The last rows of the list carry factors
  within a few percent of one; cutting the list at a hundred is a
  presentational choice, not a natural boundary.
- **Max drawdown keeps the sign the campaign stored.** Some campaigns
  record the fall as a positive number, some as a negative; the magnitude
  is the measurement.
- **Reproducibility cost the list its best-looking books.**
  Forty-three net-positive books meeting the same
  floors have no reproducible artifact left: their data files grew after
  the run, or their engine was edited after the run and the harness now
  refuses them as tampering. They are counted here, never ranked.
  One hundred and two books clear every filter; this table
  lists the first hundred of them, and the boundary is the noise floor.

The ranking script is `code/_top100_rank2.py`; the machine-readable
table is `code/_top100_rank2.json`; every cited artifact carries its
own data and engine hashes.
